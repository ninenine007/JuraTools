What the engine applies
Gain, loss and the accounting period — ss.65, 65 ter

Gain on a transfer. Sale price less the cost of acquiring the shares is income from or by reason of the business, brought into net profit for the accounting period in which the transfer falls, on the accrual basis — s.65. Where the shares are paid for with shares rather than cash, the price is the total value used in the exchange. Ruling กค 0706/7148, 25 Aug 2548.

Cost. The price paid for shares bought as an investment is the cost of that asset. Where the shares were themselves acquired in an earlier exchange, the value used in that exchange is the cost. Expenses incurred specifically and directly in acquiring the holding — a feasibility study for the project actually invested in, for instance — are capitalised into cost as expenditure of a capital nature under s.65 ter (5). Rulings กค 0702/10436, 21 Dec 2552; กค 0706/7430, 2 Sep 2548.

Loss. A loss on shares acquired to earn a benefit for the business is a deductible loss of the period in which the sale falls, and is not caught by s.65 ter (12) or (13). Shares bought with no such purpose — bought so that the loss on selling them can be deducted — are caught by s.65 ter (13). Rulings กค 0706/5694, 8 Jun 2550; กค 0706/11580, 19 Nov 2550; กค 0706/7526, 26 Jul 2550.

Movements in value without a disposal. Writing the holding up does not create income — s.65 bis (3). Writing it down is not deductible — s.65 ter (17) — and a loss from the investee's capital reduction becomes deductible only when the shares are actually sold. Rulings กค 0706/1925, 7 Mar 2549; กค 0811/02504, 19 Mar 2542; กค 0811/12651, 16 Dec 2542.

Cost method. Where the securities sold can be identified and their true cost computed, that actual cost must be used and averaging is not available. Where they cannot — scripless holdings bought in several lots — average cost, FIFO or LIFO may be chosen, and the method chosen must then be used consistently for all securities. Rulings กค 0811/01147, 29 Jan 2541; กค 0702/8322, 7 Nov 2557.

The transfer price — s.65 bis (4)

Where a company transfers an asset for a consideration below market value without reasonable cause, the assessment officer may substitute the market value at the date of transfer — s.65 bis (4).

Book value is the accepted safe harbour for unlisted shares. A price not below net asset value — total assets in the audited balance sheet for the preceding accounting period less all liabilities, divided by the registered shares — is a price with reasonable cause. Rulings กค 0811/04265, 7 May 2542; กค 0706(กม.08)/843, 7 Jul 2549; Supreme Court judgment 2722/2530.

A mirrored holding-company restructuring is reasonable cause. Where shareholders transfer their shares to a holding company and receive shares in it in the same number, at the same price and in the same proportions as they held before, the price has reasonable cause. On the later disposal of those new shares, sale price less original cost comes back into net profit under s.65. Ruling กค 0706/7148, 25 Aug 2548.

Acquiring below market value is not income at acquisition. The buyer has no income from or by reason of the business at the time of the transfer, and neither cost nor market value is brought into tax then. The exposure moves to the onward sale, where the officer may substitute the market value at the date of that transfer. Ruling กค 0811/04265, 7 May 2542.

Shares issued as the consideration — the issuer's side

A company that issues new shares and is paid for them with shares of another company receives capital, not income from or by reason of its business. It is not brought into net profit and it is not a VAT receipt. The excess over the registered value of the new shares — share premium — is capital in the same way, so the issuer bears no tax whether it issues at par or above it.

The qualification is deliberate: if new shares are issued in a manner not permitted by law, or the premium is set above what is real, or the issue is a disguise — for a grant, a subsidy or other assistance — the excess beyond what is real may be treated as income of both the issuer and the subscriber. Ruling กค 0702/3214, 12 Jun 2551.

Pricing the new shares. Because the premium is capital rather than revenue, the issuer carries no tax whether it issues at the registered value or above it — so the exchange ratio is driven by the value of what is coming in, not by the issuer's tax position. The floor is company law, not tax: shares may not be issued at less than par. Civil and Commercial Code s.1105 ¶2.

What the subscriber takes away. The new shares sit in the transferor's hands at the value used in the exchange, not at their par value. A capital increase given as consideration therefore defers the charge to the disposal of those shares rather than removing it. Ruling กค 0706/7148, 25 Aug 2548.

Entire business transfer, amalgamation, and the s.5 sattaras exemption

The shareholder's benefit. What a corporate shareholder receives when companies amalgamate, take over from one another or wind up, valued above its capital, is assessable income under s.40(4)(ฉ). In an entire business transfer this is what the shareholder receives on the transferor's liquidation — often the very shares the transferee issued — where that is worth more than what the shareholder put in.

The exemption. Section 5 sattaras of the Royal Decree issued under the Revenue Code on exemptions (No. 10) B.E. 2500, as amended by Royal Decree (No. 542) B.E. 2555, exempts a corporate shareholder on that benefit where shares are transferred in exchange for shares in the new amalgamated company or in the company taking the entire business — but only for the excess over capital, and only where the share transfer is made in the same accounting period as the amalgamation or the entire business transfer, on the conditions set by the Director-General's Notification (No. 3). The transferor company must register its dissolution and be liquidated in the accounting period of the transfer. Fail those, and the benefit is taxable.

An uplift in value is not yet income. Where shares held in the amalgamating companies come out of the amalgamation worth more — in book or market terms — than the shares given up, that uplift is s.40(4)(ฉ) income but the shareholder is taxed only on realised income: nothing is due until those shares are sold. Rulings กค 0706(กม.01)/978, 20 Oct 2548; กค 0706/9960, 28 Nov 2548; and for the liquidation route, กค 0702/10398, 21 Dec 2552.

Retained earnings carried into the new company are not paid to the old shareholders, so no one is taxed and nothing is withheld. A dividend paid out of those retained earnings before the amalgamation is s.40(4)(ข) income and is withheld on in the ordinary way.

Withholding — s.70

A corporate transferor is not withheld on for the benefit it makes over cost on a share swap, the way an individual would be. The exception is a foreign company not carrying on business in Thailand: the benefit over what it invested is assessable income under s.40(4)(ช), and the Thai payer must withhold under s.70 — including where it pays by issuing new shares rather than in cash.

Where a double tax agreement gives the right to tax capital gains to the state of residence, there is nothing to withhold. Ruling กค 0702(กม)/8852, 10 Oct 2556 — Singapore transferor, article 13 of the treaty, no withholding by the Thai issuer.
