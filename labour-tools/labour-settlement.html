Employee Facts
Termination (s.118 ¶2) happens only when the employer stops both the work and the wages. Garden leave (released from work, wages still paid): employment runs on — enter the day the notice takes effect and answer No.
Wage = the last rate at dismissal, counting only ค่าจ้าง items (a fixed cost-of-living allowance counts; welfare items like utility or child allowances do not). Daily rate: monthly ÷ 30, weekly ÷ 7, per-period ÷ its days. Piece-rate wages are entered per claim below — each claim treats them differently.
Claim Details
Work after the last settled pay day is still owed as wages (ค่าจ้างค้างจ่าย): time wages run from the day after "paid through" to the last day of employment (auto-counted in calendar days — override for a daily-paid employee who did not work every day), plus piece output produced in that stub. For monthly staff the default measure is monthly ÷ 30 per day; monthly ÷ actual days in the month is selectable below. Due within 3 days of termination (s.70 ¶2). Where the stub falls inside the s.118 window, the same piece wages also count in the severance look-back.
Days from the termination effective date through the date lawful notice would end (s.17), both counted. Use the dedicated pay-in-lieu tool to derive this from the wage cycle. Computed on the time-based wage only — piece-rate output cannot be projected forward.
Commission or output pay actually earned in the tier period before the termination effective date. Added to the time-based severance.
Piece-rate employees: leave pay adds the average daily wage of the last pay period before termination (ค่าจ้างโดยเฉลี่ยในวันทำงานในงวดการจ่ายค่าจ้างก่อนวันหยุด, s.60) to the time-based daily rate.
Discretionary s.49 estimate only — off by default below.
